# Why the rules in CLAUDE.md are what they are

**This file holds no rules.** `CLAUDE.md` is the operative manual and the only place a rule lives; this is what each one cost and which run paid for it. Two files holding the same rule drift, and the drift is invisible because both look authoritative — so nothing here tells a session what to do.

A rule whose reason you cannot see is a rule you are about to simplify away. That is the whole purpose of this file.

Split out of `CLAUDE.md` on 16.09.2026. That file was about 20'500 tokens and every session read it whole before doing anything; roughly half of it was this. The rules stayed, the stories moved, and the section names on both sides match so a pointer lands where it says it does.

## Current state

**The table carried concept counts and compile status until 15.08.2026, and both rows had gone stale**: 105 concepts against 211, and "nothing compiled" against 12. Nothing kept them true, and this is the one file every session reads end to end, so a wrong number there is read more often than anywhere else.

The counts moved to where a run rewrites them anyway. Same reasoning as `_ACTION-ITEMS.md` being generated rather than authored: two files holding one state drift, and the drift is invisible because both look authoritative.

**Checking prose against measurement was offered and declined** in favour of the blunter ban, on the precedent of this table: there is nothing left to drift because there is nothing left to be wrong. That is where the count-belongs-in-a-generated-file rule came from.

**The split of the three employer bases, 15.08.2026.** One combined knowledge base held all three as chapter directories inside one OKF bundle. They were split because two are closed archives and one is not, and the operating rules differ accordingly: `Alpha_kb` has an ingest protocol, a live coverage queue and concepts that can go stale, while a run that changes anything under the other two is a mistake rather than an update.

**The chapter level went with it.** Inside a single-employer knowledge base it distinguished nothing, and a vestigial directory level is worse than none: every later run has to remember it exists.

**Archive sizes are snapshots.** The three OneNote layers measured 1'625, 2'341 and 2'596 pages on 22.08.2026 — 6'562 together. Alpha was 10'561 before its export was deleted and replaced on 21.08.2026. `COVERAGE.md` has superseded them since.

## Read this file whole

**Owner's instruction of 15.08.2026**, after a session skipped the viewer-regeneration rule while working from a targeted read of three other sections.

The ones that get missed are the ones a task-shaped search does not think to look for, and they are exactly the ones written down because they were missed before.

## Editing CLAUDE.md

**Until 15.08.2026 the librarian was forbidden to touch `CLAUDE.md` at all.** That rule cost more than it protected. In one evening it produced three separate hand-overs — a dead archive path in one knowledge base's own manual that was the vault's only outstanding defect, the promotion rule with nowhere to live, and the read rule — each of which stalled until the owner said "please do the edit".

**A rule that is suspended every time it binds is not a safeguard; it is a delay with a ritual attached.**

What has not changed is why the file matters. It is the one file every session reads end to end, so a wrong line there is read more often than a wrong line anywhere else, and it is the layer no audit was checking until the vault-root `assertions.yaml` was added on 15.08.2026. **Consultation is the check this file gets instead of a script.**

**An unattended run has nobody to ask**, and consultation that nobody answers is not consultation.

## One Outputs folder

Each knowledge base kept its own `Outputs/` until 21.08.2026, which meant a question spanning two employers had no correct home and was filed by hand into the larger one. **Eleven of the twenty reports on record drew on more than one knowledge base**, so the per-base split was answering a question the corpus does not ask. The register's Scope column carries what the folder used to.

## Drafts

**Settled 15.08.2026 as AI-2026-08-15-4**, after nine concepts were found sitting at `draft` for no reason anyone could name — which makes the field decorative, and a decorative field is worse than an absent one because it looks like information.

`status` is lifecycle and `verified` is trust, and they answer different questions. So the rule is not about whether a concept's facts have been confirmed.

**The rule was read off practice rather than invented.** One group of concepts already worked this way: three concepts distilling a closed published document sat at `stable`, and the fourth sat at `draft` under a heading naming the two claims it could not support. Nine system-card concepts distilling a published PDF, every claim footnoted and nothing unsettled named, were promoted the same day.

**Saying a thing twice in two fields is the duplication this vault keeps relearning about.** A concept being unverified is already said by the absence of `verified`.

**The drafts predating the rule were cleared on 16.09.2026**: a script promoted the ones giving no reason and four readers took the rest, leaving a handful across the vault, each naming what is unsettled. Every knowledge base opts in to the check from that day (`Gamma_kb` AI-2026-09-15-3).

## Testimony

**This matters more than it sounds.** A large share of the open questions in `questions.md` are not research tasks at all: what an acronym expanded to, who initials belong to, whether a contract was signed, what a folder was for. The archive cannot answer them and never will. The owner can, in seconds, and the answer is worth more than the pages around it because nothing else records it.

The pattern was used first on 09.08.2026. The `_testimony/` transcripts of 09. and 10.08.2026 cover all three employers, which is why they sit at the vault root rather than inside one knowledge base.

## Promotion

**Owner's decision of 15.09.2026.** Until then a report could only be *proposed* for promotion and then waited for approval, and **no step asked for it**. No compile read `Outputs/`; two health checks named a waiting report and raised nothing, because the rule said the owner approves; and on 15.09.2026 five reports had stood at `pending review` for 12 to 29 days.

**The checking step was paid for by the Glenfis report of 14.09.2026**, which carried two statements its own source contradicted.

**The `verified` key goes because the owner confirmed the text as it stood**, and a finding written into a concept is a change to a claim.

**Why a report with no row is a defect** rather than untidiness: the same question asked twice is the stronger of the two promotion signals, and the register is the only place a second asking can be seen.

## Audit reports in their own folder

**The check reads the register rather than the file names.** A name test would pass `2026-08-09_contradiction-sweep.md`, which is an audit and says nothing about health checks, and would fail a question report that happened to ask about one.

## The defined read

**Settled 15.08.2026 as AI-2026-08-15-7**, replacing "every run reads every concept".

That earlier rule was itself a correction. A cheap delta most months and a full audit quarterly was removed on 09.08.2026, because the scope had been written as though it governed the scripted checks too — which would have let a moved source folder go unnoticed for a quarter — and because "three concepts sampled at random" had no principled basis. The trigger for revisiting it was stated as roughly 500 concepts.

**The trigger arrived early, and by size rather than count.** One bundle passed it at 219 concepts and 1.8 MB of body text, because the compile writes long concepts. The health check of 15.08.2026 read 21 concepts end to end and machine-scanned all 219 — so the runs were already truncating, and the only thing making that visible was a CHANGELOG line naming what had been read. **That is the honest version of the wrong thing, and it stays wrong until the rule changes.**

**The three arms do different jobs.** Changed-since catches new work, which is where fresh error lives. Most-linked catches the concepts where a wrong claim propagates furthest — a claim in a concept with 53 inbound links is a claim the bundle leans on. The cluster arm is the sweep the skill already required. Random sampling was withdrawn in August 2026.

**A run that picks its own scope will always pick what it had budget for**, which is why the derivation was taken out of the run's hands and why the previous date is read rather than remembered.

## Withdrawn action items

**A `withdrawn` row is memory about the audit rather than about the corpus.** It is what stops the same false positive being rediscovered every quarter.

## The scheduled task

The owner withdrew the automatic health check on 28.08.2026, while moving the vault's day-to-day operation to Claude Code, after `/loop` proved unable to hold a monthly cadence and a launchd agent was weighed and declined.

**The Cowork task's facts are kept because they were expensive to establish**, and because the task may still exist. Settled 09.08.2026 as AI-2026-08-09-21, after the cron and the reporting rule were each found written down twice with the copies disagreeing.

**It was verified rather than assumed.** A one-time read-only task was fired through the same scheduler, in a fresh session with no memory of the one that created it, and reported: `00_Cerebrum/CLAUDE.md readable: yes`, `.md files under Combined_kb/Wiki: 149`, `files changed: none`, `VERDICT: a scheduled run CAN reach the vault unattended`.

**The cron is local time.** The skill's setup section assumes UTC and gives `0 7 1 * *`; entered here that would fire at 07:00 rather than 09:00. Because it is local it stays 09:00 through both halves of the year, so there is nothing to adjust twice.

**The EPERM note exists because the opening check would otherwise abort a healthy run.** In that environment `Read` returns `EPERM` and `Glob` returns nothing for the the cloud drive path, while the bash workspace mount reads the same files cleanly — a macOS Full Disk Access matter, not a missing file. The earlier wording said "on this machine", which reads as a standing fact about the Mac and would send a local session around a limit it does not have; confirmed 22.08.2026 that a local session reads those paths with `Read` normally.

**A cloud session cannot create the task, and the reason is structural.** Tested 09.08.2026: a task created from a cloud session and fired on demand opened a session with no `mcp__remote-devices__*` tools registered at all — not denied, absent. Its verdict: `device bridge tools present: no`, `00_Cerebrum reachable: no`. That task was deleted the same day. Rescheduling, retrying, or moving the hour changes nothing. **A task that fails every month is worse than no task, because the vault then looks audited and is not.**

## Bridge sessions

**Proven 09.08.2026**: a session working through the device bridge cannot run git in the vault. The bridge forbids file deletion, git cannot remove its own lock files, and a repository wedges permanently after one commit.

That is what the lock rule cost on 15.08.2026 — a health-check sub-agent ran `git status --short` because the skill's own step 1 told it to, and the owner's commit failed three and a half hours later with a stale zero-byte lock owned by the session VM.

**Reading these as ambient constraints is its own failure.** A local session came to record the git history purge as impossible on 22.08.2026 — true of a bridge session, false of the one that could have run it — and the work sat undone as a result.

## Git rules

**The CHANGELOG pairing** comes from j4k, where docs drifting from behaviour are actively misleading. Here the CHANGELOG *is* the behaviour record.

**One entry per commit was settled 22.08.2026**, after the three-script documentation was appended to that day's existing entry: the heading was already spent on the previous commit, so the commit that followed had no heading to open with and got an invented subject instead.

**The unit of a commit was settled 29.08.2026**, correcting a per-change reading that split one piece of work in half. murmur commits *Parakeet as a second recognition engine* as a single commit across six files, and *A wedged microphone now stops, instead of burning a core in silence* as another.

**Without the entry's opening paragraph** the commit body is the diffstat and the audit result, which says what changed and nothing about why.

**The credential rule came from a real leak.** The vault is private and the corpus is not sanitised — that is *Scope: everything counts*, and it stands. The repository is a different boundary, and it had no equivalent guard until a certificate passphrase reached GitHub on 22.08.2026. Purging history reduces exposure and cannot undo it.

**the cloud drive and git coexist with a known friction**: `.git/` is thousands of small files that the cloud drive will sync. That is half the reason the GitHub remote exists.

## Scripts

Each of these exists because something went wrong that prose had failed to prevent.

- **`verify.py`** — the `generated.at` check sat documented and unexecuted through four clean-looking runs; on its first real run the script caught two defect classes the hand-written checks had been blind to. Since 15.08.2026 it checks an index by asking whether `reindex.py` would change it: the old test passed seven truncated descriptions sitting in one index while reporting the bundle clean, and could not see an entry left behind by a concept that no longer exists.
- **Proving a new check** — done first for the vault-root `assertions.yaml` on 15.08.2026, and that is the standard rather than the exception. Adapted from j4k, where the rule repeatedly caught guards that were wrong on the first attempt. **Proving a confirmation guard by removing it performs the guarded action**: done on 16.09.2026 and it cost 17 GB of model weights and the download again.
- **`reindex.py`** — a compile always leaves indexes stale by exactly the number of concepts it added: 265 entries across two bundles on 15.08.2026. Detection had always existed and the repair was manual, which is why it accumulated — **a repair nobody automates is a repair that gets skipped.** The no-reorder rule exists because three different orders were already in use and each had been chosen on purpose.
- **`visualize.py` and `viewer-check.js`** — every viewer defect to date was caught by the owner while the generator ran clean. The check is the render-and-look habit made executable. The regeneration rule is the owner's instruction of 14.08.2026, and it was missed again the same week by a run that followed a per-KB protocol which did not carry it — **a rule that lives only in the vault file depends on someone remembering to look there.**
- **`viewer-server.py`** — the page is one static file opened from disk and can reach nothing on its own. The origin rule exists because a web page you happened to be visiting would otherwise reach a process that can write to the vault and call the model.
- **`mermaid-check.py`** — on 18.08.2026 three diagrams shipped with the caveat that they could not be rendered: npm answered 403 in both the session container and the device VM, and the CDNs were tunnel-blocked. The way through is the owner's own browser. All three were confirmed against Mermaid v11.16.1.
- **`namescan.py`** — replaces a grep procedure that was wrong by an order of magnitude twice (AI-2026-08-09-22, AI-2026-08-10-1).
- **`coverage.py`** — the old measure asked whether a concept existed for a scope, which is a question about the bundle: it reported Alpha as fully compiled while 10'122 of 10'561 pages had never been cited (15.08.2026). Before 31.08.2026 the archive folder name was hardcoded, so a bundle whose corpus arrived elsewhere measured as having no corpus at all — exit 0, no table, 262 pages invisible, **which is coverage-by-assertion arriving through the one script written to prevent it.**
- **`prepare-batch.py`** — these archives are rolling notes, so only 14 per cent of Alpha lines and 36 per cent of the remaining Gamma and Beta lines had never appeared before. Reading every page whole means reading the same line several times over; a packed batch runs about a third the size.
- **`merge-appends.py`** — its four historical bugs are documented in the code.
- **`fix-runons.py`** — a line ending in a footnote reference followed straight by a new sentence renders as one paragraph, so two claims with two different sources read as one claim. Because it is a rendering fault rather than a truth fault it is a finding rather than a defect, **which is how 147 of them accumulated in `Alpha_kb` before anyone repaired any.** The cause was `merge-appends.py` joining an appended chunk with a single newline; that is fixed at the source, and this clears what the old behaviour already wrote.
- **`fix-depth.py`** — `../../OneNote/...` is right from `Wiki/<group>/x.md` and wrong one level deeper. Fixed by hand twice and back a third time on the Alpha rebuild of 21.08.2026.
- **`plan-batches.py`** — the Gamma plan was made by hand and its batch count was wrong in two files for a day.
- **`inventory.py`** — had no generator until 15.08.2026 and went stale, and agents duplicated concepts under second names as a result.
- **`attachment-register.py`** — the worst of three omissions: the file it writes carries a header saying it is generated and must not be edited, and the manual named no generator.
- **`check-login.py`** — the command line signs in separately from the desktop app and dies after about a month with nothing to say so. The first sign on 16.09.2026 was a job failing, three weeks after the token had expired on 27.08.2026. macOS notifications were tested on this Mac on 16.09.2026 and never appeared: the process posting them is not one macOS has granted notification rights. Nothing was built on them, because a reminder that can fail is worse than none.
- **`set-icon.sh`** — a custom Finder icon lives in the file's resource fork, and git tracks a file's bytes and not its forks.
- **`sync.sh`** — the commit step used to be a block of shell pasted into Terminal. On 22.08.2026 a 48-line message pasted cleanly, reached `.git/COMMIT_EDITMSG`, and produced no commit at all, with no error anyone had kept. **A script fails in one place and says why; a pasted heredoc fails silently and leaves you comparing hashes.**
- **`build-skill.py`** — the health-check skill spent its first week with no tracked source, edited inside an ephemeral container with only the zip returned, so every change showed in git as a blob of a different size. Since 28.08.2026 the `.claude/skills/` symlinks are a third path to the same files and still one source: before them, a `claude` session in Terminal saw no skills at all.
- **The vault-root `assertions.yaml`** — the per-KB files guard concepts; nothing guarded the files above them, and `verify.py` audited only `<KB>/Wiki/`. **That gap is how a stale concept count sat in the *Current state* table through at least two runs while every audit ran green.** Proven to fire the day it was added: reintroducing a count produced a defect and exit 1, and its absence exit 0.
- **The finding clock** — standing noise trains the reader to skip the findings block.

## Output presentation

**Got wrong on 09.08.2026 and spotted from a screenshot.** A `computer://` link renders as its own file card in the Cowork interface, so delivering a file *and* linking it produced two cards for one file, and the second carried a save-destination prompt that had nothing to do with the vault.

**The earlier version of the rule said to do both**, on the reasoning that the page is for reading now and the link for finding later. That reasoning was fine and the implementation was not: the interface renders both as cards, so the belt-and-braces version just looks like a bug.

**`computer://` stopped existing on 28.08.2026** with the move to Claude Code, where a path relative to the vault root is already clickable. Three manuals and the template went on restating the Cowork form for eighteen days.

The page is for reading now; the filename is what you search for in Obsidian in six months.

## The compile skill's numbers

Measured 15.08.2026 with opus agents, one batch each: one batch alone 160k tokens for 55 pages, a wave of eight 1.55–1.65M for about 440 pages, the whole Gamma archive 4.71M for 1'625 pages, Beta 7.26M for 2'341. Alpha was rebuilt greenfield on 22.08.2026 — 2'596 pages, 9 waves, about 9M tokens. **Greenfield runs dearer per batch**, around 250k against 200k: with an empty bundle an agent writes many concepts instead of appending to existing ones.

**Sleep is the binding constraint on a long run, not tokens.** Two Alpha waves were destroyed mid-flight when the machine slept, at roughly 200k per killed agent. Nothing already written was lost — concepts go down as `set -C` heredocs and are atomic — but the reading was paid for twice.

**The skill exists because nobody should reconstruct the method from a CHANGELOG.** The sub-agent prompt is the part the scripts cannot hold.
